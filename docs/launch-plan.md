# ToonTails — 90-Day Launch Plan

*(formerly PupTV — domain **toontails.tv** is pending; the live product is at **puptv.vercel.app** until the switch is made.)*

ToonTails creates AI cartoon episodes starring your own dog, auto-delivered to a YouTube channel that plays on any TV. $1 of every episode sold is pledged to dog rescues. This is a passion side-project among several businesses Chad runs — not a full-time job — so the whole plan is built around one hard constraint:

> **Chad spends no more than 1–2 hours a week on this.** AI generates, drafts, and schedules. Humans click approve.

**Pricing:** Single episode — $2.99 Good tier / $4.99 Great tier · Three-pack — $9.99 · Six-episode season — $19.99. ($2.99 "Good" = the Budget animation stack, $4.99 "Great" = the current Standard stack, per `docs/economics-and-structure.md` §1.2.) **$1 of every episode** is pledged to the rescue the customer picks — or to the rescue that referred them, see the outreach playbook below.

**Team:**

| Who | Role |
|---|---|
| Chad | Approvals, money, final say |
| Chad's wife | Social media presence — posting cadence, community replies |
| Grayson Smith | Outreach — rescue partnerships, local contacts |
| AI (Cowork) | Generates content, drafts outreach, schedules posts, compiles metrics |

**Budget:** $500–$1,000 seed for the first 90 days (full allocation in Section 5).

**Total Chad time across all 90 days, per this plan: ~5.5 hours (~25 min/week average)** — comfortably inside the 1–2 hr/week ceiling, with slack built in for real-world friction. Week-by-week Chad totals are called out below so this stays checkable.

This plan operationalizes the research already done in `docs/economics-and-structure.md` (economics, charity structure, marketing math), `docs/marketing-copy.md` (site copy, pricing, FAQ), and `docs/competitors.md` (landscape) — it doesn't repeat that research, it turns it into a checklist.

## Section 2: Rescue-Partnership Outreach Playbook (Grayson)

### How to pick your 10 target rescues

Priority order:

1. **TX first, two clusters** — Dallas/Fort Worth metro (near Purple Sage) and Texoma/Grayson County (near The Henderson, Pottsboro). Places Chad can literally drive to if a partnership ever needs an in-person touch.
2. **Foster-based or small volunteer-run rescue over mega-shelters.** A 5-person foster network can say yes on a phone call this week; a big city shelter routes the ask through a marketing committee for two months. Aim for roughly 200–5,000 social followers — big enough to have a real audience, small enough to move fast.
3. **Active on Facebook** (posts at least weekly) — that's where their donor base actually is (57% of 65+ Americans use Facebook per the economics doc's Pew citation), and where the free demo episode will actually get seen and shared.
4. **Already has decent photos of adoptable dogs** on their page — makes the free demo episode easy to produce without arranging a special photo shoot.
5. **Not already running a competing "buy this, we donate" storefront tie-in** (rare, but worth a quick check).
6. **Legit registered nonprofit.** Quick gut-check only (full legal vetting is Phase-1 lawyer work per the economics doc §2.6): search "[rescue name] EIN" or look them up on the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (irs.gov/teos) before signing anything.

**Where to find candidates:** Facebook search ("Dallas dog rescue," "Denton county dog rescue," "Grayson county animal rescue," "Pottsboro TX dog rescue," "Texoma dog rescue"), Instagram hashtags (#dallasdogrescue, #texomarescue, #northtexasrescue), Petfinder.org's shelter/rescue directory filtered to TX zips near 75240 and 75076, and word of mouth — every rescue that responds knows others in their network worth asking. Build a list of 15–20 candidates to land 10 signed partners; expect roughly a 50–65% yes rate from small orgs when the ask is genuinely free.

Two known regional players came up in our competitive research and are worth checking as backstop/prestige targets (verify current contact info yourself — these are leads, not confirmed partners): Operation Kindness (Carrollton) and SPCA of Texas affiliates. Prioritize the scrappier foster networks first for speed; these bigger names can come later once the playbook is proven.

## Section 3: Social Engine (Wife)

### Platform priorities

1. **Facebook groups + Instagram Reels — first priority.** This is where dog owners 55+ (the primary buyer) and the gifting demographic actually are (57% of 65+ Americans use Facebook, 64% use YouTube — per the economics doc's Pew citation).
2. **TikTok — second priority.** Add once the Facebook/IG cadence feels easy — roughly Week 7+ in the plan above. Same content, repurposed, no extra production.

### Cadence: 3 posts/week

Monday / Wednesday / Friday. AI (Cowork automation) generates a weekly content pack — 3 short clips + captions, pulled from existing Dutch episodes or newly-produced ones — every Sunday night. Wife's job Mon/Wed/Fri: open the pack, pick the clip, hit post. **~15 minutes/week** if she posts captions as-is, more like 20–25 if she tweaks the wording to sound like her own voice (recommended — authenticity is the whole point, see etiquette below).

### 10 concrete first-post ideas (all before/after photo → cartoon reveal format)

1. "Dutch, before and after" — split-screen: real photo of Dutch next to his cartoon character reveal.
2. "POV: I turned my dog into a Pixar character" — Reels trend audio + reveal.
3. Grandma/gift angle: "Sent this to my mom's TV and she cried" — screen recording of the YouTube channel playing on a living-room TV.
4. "Rate my dog's cartoon glow-up 1–10" — engagement-bait caption, invites comments.
5. Side-by-side of 3 different dogs' cartoon versions — "which one's your favorite?"
6. Behind-the-scenes: "here's the 5 photos I sent vs. what came back" — process transparency.
7. Rescue-partner reveal: "Look what we made of Biscuit — [Rescue Name]'s most adoptable pup" (once the first demo episode lands — this is the single biggest lever; tag the rescue).
8. "Things my dog does in real life vs. his cartoon adventures" — funny juxtaposition.
9. Gift-occasion framing: "the gift that made my mom's whole year — for $2.99" (seasonal; reuse the pattern for any dog-owner holiday).
10. Charity framing: "Every one of these funds a rescue dog somewhere — here's this week's ledger" — pairs a cute reveal with the mission without leading with the ask.

### Community-group etiquette (read before posting in any Facebook group)

Dog breed and rescue Facebook groups almost universally **ban direct ads and self-promotion** — posting "Buy my product!" gets deleted, and repeat offenses can get her banned from the group entirely. The workaround that actually works: **post it as herself, not as ToonTails.** "Look what I made of my Frenchie!" with the before/after reveal — mention where she got it made only if someone asks in the comments, or if the group has an explicit self-promo thread/day. Golden rule: the post should look exactly like something she'd share even if she weren't involved in the business at all — genuine, a little proud, zero hard sell.

### Kill / scale criteria — check these at Day 60 and Day 90

- **Rescue referral channel:** if rescue-referred customers total **fewer than 10 by Day 60**, stop investing outreach time in new rescues (keep existing signed partners, just don't chase more) — the channel isn't converting fast enough to justify Grayson's time.
- **Influencer seeding:** if **fewer than 3 of the 20–30** gifted accounts post anything within 3 weeks of receiving their episode, stop gifting — the format isn't landing with that audience segment.
- **Organic social:** if engagement (likes + comments + shares) is **flat or declining after 8 weeks** of consistent 3x/week posting, don't add TikTok as planned in Week 7 — fix the Facebook/IG format first instead of spreading thinner.
- **Paid boosted posts:** if CAC comes in **above $15**, don't scale further this quarter — the $2.99–$4.99 singles can't absorb it (contribution per single is only ~$1.65–2 after COGS and pledge, per the economics doc). If CAC comes in **under $10**, that's workable against the $19.99 season or a future Deluxe SKU — put the remaining ad budget behind it.
- **Phase 1 vs Phase 1.5:** if the pledge run-rate by Day 90 projects to **more than ~$4,000/year** flowing to rescues, start the Phase 1.5 steps from the economics doc (Massachusetts/Alabama commercial co-venturer registration if advertising there; California registration or compliance-in-lieu). If under that, **stay exactly where you are** — no new legal/compliance work needed yet.
