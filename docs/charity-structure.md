# Charity Structure Options for PupTV

**IMPORTANT:** This document is research for discussion with a lawyer, **not legal advice**. Consult a tax attorney and nonprofit specialist before making a decision.

---

## Option A: Own 501(c)(3) Nonprofit

### Timeline
- **Form 1023-EZ (simplified):** 2–4 weeks approval
- **Form 1023 (full):** 3–6 months approval
- **State incorporation:** 1–2 weeks (parallel)
- **Total:** 2–6 months to tax-exempt status

### Costs
- **State incorporation:** $50–$200 (varies by state)
- **IRS Form 1023-EZ:** $275 filing fee
- **IRS Form 1023 (full):** $600 filing fee
- **Attorney fees (optional but recommended):** $1,500–$5,000
- **Annual Form 990-N (e-filing if under $50K):** Free
- **Annual Form 990-EZ (if $50K–$200K):** Free filing with accountant: ~$500–$1,500/year
- **Total out-of-pocket:** $300–$500 (DIY) to $3,000+ (with attorney)

**Source:** [Cost to Start a Nonprofit in 2026](https://www.getalignmint.org/blog/cost-to-start-a-nonprofit), [How Much Does It Cost to Start a Nonprofit? 2026 Guide](https://www.incauthority.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-nonprofit/)

### Pros
- **Full tax deductibility:** Donors can claim 100% of gifts as charitable deductions
- **Marketing power:** "PupTV is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit" carries trust
- **Grant eligibility:** Can apply directly for foundation grants
- **Governance control:** Full board control over mission and operations
- **Can say "100% of profits":** All revenue stays in the organization

### Cons
- **Long approval timeline:** 3–6 months can be slow to launch
- **Ongoing compliance:** Annual Form 990 filing, state annual reports, director/officer disclosures
- **Setup burden:** Bylaws, conflict-of-interest policy, meeting minutes, board meetings
- **Limited revenue model:** Can't distribute profits to founders
- **Founder lock-in:** Once established, mission can't easily change

### Best For
- Long-term commitment to rescue funding (5+ years)
- Ability to wait 6 months for launch
- Plan to raise significant donations or grants
- Willing to manage ongoing compliance

## Option B: Fiscal Sponsorship

### Timeline
- **Application:** 1–4 weeks
- **Approval & setup:** 1–3 weeks
- **Total:** 2–7 weeks (can accept donations immediately upon approval)

### Costs
- **Annual sponsorship fee:** 5–15% of incoming donations/revenue
  - Average: **10%**
  - Example: If you raise $100K annually, pay sponsor $10K
- **Typical minimum:** $12K–$24K annual revenue requirement
- **Monthly minimum (some sponsors):** $100–$500/month

**Common Fiscal Sponsors:**

| Sponsor | Fee | Min Annual | Services | Focus |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------|-------|
| [Tides Center](https://www.tides.org/fiscal-sponsorship-at-tides/) | 9% | Not specified | Full back-office, HR, grant admin | Social justice, equity, tech |
| [Community Initiatives](https://communityinitiatives.org/learn/fees-and-minimums/) | 10% or 15% (gov't) | $24K | Payroll, insurance, compliance, grants | General nonprofit |
| [NOPI](https://www.thenopi.org/fiscal-sponsorship) | $100/month | $12K/year | Insurance, compliance, platform tools, support | General nonprofit |

**Source:** [Fiscal Sponsorship Fee Structures Explained](https://www.getalignmint.org/blog/fiscal-sponsorship-fee-structures), [Tides Center Fiscal Sponsorship](https://www.tides.org/fiscal-sponsorship-at-tides/), [Community Initiatives](https://communityinitiatives.org/learn/fees-and-minimums/), [NOPI Fiscal Sponsorship](https://www.thenopi.org/fiscal-sponsorship)

### Pros
- **Fast launch:** Weeks, not months
- **Immediate tax-deductibility:** Donors' gifts are tax-deductible from day one (sponsor is the 501(c)(3))
- **Low upfront cost:** No filing fees or incorporation costs
- **Administrative relief:** Sponsor handles compliance, payroll, insurance, annual reporting
- **Grant access:** Can apply for grants under sponsor's 501(c)(3) status
- **Flexibility:** If the project fails, unwind cleanly without dissolving an org

### Cons
- **Ongoing fee (5–15%):** Reduces program funding
- **Less marketing power:** "Fiscally sponsored by X" is weaker than "We are a 501(c)(3)"
- **Reduced autonomy:** Sponsor has oversight of spending, hiring, mission alignment
- **Minimum revenue requirement:** May not accept projects under $12K–$24K annual revenue
- **Limited to say "100% of profits":** Must acknowledge sponsor fee
- **Sponsor risk:** If sponsor loses 501(c)(3) status or closes, project must find new sponsor

### Best For
- Quick launch (2–7 weeks)
- Uncertain revenue model at first
- Want to test market before committing to full nonprofit
- Small team without compliance bandwidth
- Plan to raise <$24K annually in early stage

## Option C: For-Profit with Binding Donation Pledge

### Timeline
- **Business registration:** 1–2 weeks
- **Setup:** 1 week
- **Total:** 1–2 weeks (fastest option)

### Costs
- **Incorporation/LLC:** $50–$300 (state filing)
- **Optional legal review:** $500–$2,000
- **Annual report:** $0–$100
- **Total:** $50–$2,000

### Model: Pledge 1%

**Pledge 1%** allows companies to commit 1% of profits, equity, product, or employee time to social impact.

- **Structure:** For-profit business pledges % to nonprofits
- **Donors NOT tax-deductible:** Personal donors can't claim deduction (company's pledge is business donation, not individual deduction)
- **Marketing:** Join 8,500+ companies pledging at [pledge1percent.org](https://www.pledge1percent.org/)
- **Binding:** You choose pledge level, but it's a public commitment

**Alternative: 1% for the Planet**

- **Focus:** Environmental causes only
- **Requirement:** Certify that you donate 1% of annual sales to environmental nonprofits
- **Donors:** Get corporate ESG credibility, not tax deductions
- **5,500+ certified companies** have given $580M+

**Source:** [A global movement for corporate impact - Pledge 1%](https://www.pledge1percent.org/), [1% for the Planet](https://www.onepercentfortheplanet.org/membership)

### Pros
- **Fastest setup:** 1–2 weeks to launch
- **Full control:** No regulatory oversight, no board requirements
- **Profitable:** Founders can take salary and distributions
- **Marketing:** "We pledge 1% of profits to dog rescue" has positive brand angle
- **Flexibility:** Can change pledge level or recipient nonprofits
- **Tax-deductible to company:** Company donation is business expense, reducing taxes

### Cons
- **Donations NOT tax-deductible to donors:** If someone buys PupTV video and it donates to rescue, **they cannot claim a deduction**
- **Marketing friction:** "We pledge 1%" is weaker than "100% of profits go to charity"
- **Regulatory risk:** If pledge isn't paid out, reputational damage
- **Harder to raise donations:** Donors preferring tax deductions won't contribute
- **Less grant access:** Most foundation grants require 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsorship status

### Best For
- Want to keep business control and profit potential
- Fast launch with low overhead
- Founders comfortable being personally liable
- Plan to generate revenue from product sales, not donations
- B2B or subscription model (users = dog owners, not donors)

## Option D: Donor-Advised Fund (DAF)

### Timeline
- **DAF creation:** 1–2 weeks
- **Fund setup:** 1–2 days
- **Total:** 1–2 weeks

### Costs
- **DAF account opening:** $0–$500 (depending on DAF provider)
- **Annual administration:** $0–$150
- **Contribution grants to nonprofits:** No fee (donors recommend, DAF processes)
- **Total:** $0–$650 one-time + $0–$150/year

**How it works:**
1. Founder contributes money to a DAF account at Fidelity, Schwab, or similar
2. Founder takes immediate tax deduction for full contribution
3. Founder recommends grants to specific nonprofits (up to annual payout requirement)
4. DAF processes grants; nonprofits receive funding
5. PupTV itself doesn't hold the money—the DAF does

**Source:** [Fiscal Sponsorship (Uncle Kam)](https://unclekam.com/tax-write-offs/deductions/fiscal-sponsorship/)

### Pros
- **Personal tax deduction:** Founder gets immediate tax break
- **Simple setup:** No nonprofit formation or incorporation needed
- **Flexible timing:** Can contribute bulk amount in high-income year, grant out over time
- **Full control:** Founder decides which nonprofits to support
- **No annual reporting:** DAF handles compliance

### Cons
- **Founder-centric:** This is a personal charitable savings account, not an organization
- **Doesn't work for fundraising:** Can't take donations from customers into the DAF
- **No "PupTV is a charity" messaging:** DAF is behind the scenes
- **Only works if founder has excess cash:** Requires founder to fund the rescue giving
- **Not suitable if PupTV is the fundraiser:** This option only works if PupTV is profitable and founder donates surplus

### Best For
- Founder has existing wealth and wants to donate profits
- Don't need customer donations to flow into charitable account
- Want personal tax deduction on contributions
- Want simplicity over nonprofit identity

## Decision Framework

**Choose Own 501(c)(3) if:**
- Plan to raise significant donations or foundation grants (>$50K/year)
- Willing to wait 3–6 months for launch
- Want "nonprofit" branding to be central
- Long-term commitment (5+ years)
- Can manage ongoing compliance (board meetings, annual filings)

**Choose Fiscal Sponsorship if:**
- Want to launch in <8 weeks
- Uncertain if revenue model will work yet
- Plan to raise $12K–$50K annually
- Want tax-deductible donations without incorporation overhead
- Can tolerate 10% administrative fee

**Choose For-Profit + Pledge if:**
- Plan to generate revenue from customers (subscriptions, purchases)
- Founders willing to be personally liable
- Prioritize speed and control over tax-deductible branding
- Budget model: sell to customers, pledge % to rescue nonprofits
- Don't expect direct donations to PupTV

**Choose DAF if:**
- PupTV is a profitable for-profit company
- Founder personally donates proceeds to rescues
- Don't need customer donations to be tax-deductible
- Want simplicity

## Next Steps

1. **Consult a lawyer.** Different structures have tax and liability implications.
2. **Model your revenue.** Will you rely on donations, customer subscriptions, or founder gifting?
3. **Decide launch timeline.** Can you wait 6 months (501c3) or need 2 weeks (for-profit)?
4. **Choose your pitch.** Will marketing emphasize "nonprofit" vs. "for-profit with purpose"?
5. **Set donation mechanics.** If fiscal sponsorship or 501c3, how will donations flow? If for-profit, how will pledges be tracked?
